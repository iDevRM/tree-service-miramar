The Work Itself

What Is Stump Grinding?

A stump grinder is a machine with a rotating steel wheel set with carbide teeth. It chews the stump down below grade, turning wood into a pile of chips that can be raked into the hole or hauled off.

Standard grinding takes the stump six to eight inches below the surrounding soil, which is enough to plant grass over and forget about it. If you intend to put a new tree, a fence post, a paver patio, or a pool deck in the same spot, we go deeper — usually twelve to eighteen inches — and tell you what that costs before starting.

It's worth knowing what grinding does not do: it doesn't remove the root system. The lateral roots stay in the ground and decompose over several years. For nearly every residential situation that's completely fine.

The Alternative

Grinding or Full Removal — Which Do You Need?

Full stump removal means excavating the stump and its main root plate out of the ground entirely. It requires heavy equipment, leaves a large crater that has to be backfilled, and typically costs three to five times what grinding does.

Grinding is right for the overwhelming majority of Miramar properties: you want grass, mulch, or a garden bed where the tree was, and you want it done in an afternoon without a machine tearing up your yard to get there.

Full removal is worth it in a narrow set of cases — when you're pouring a foundation or a structural slab in that exact spot, when a pool is going in over the footprint, or when the root plate is actively lifting a driveway or a wall and grinding the stump alone won't solve it. We'll tell you which one you're actually dealing with rather than upselling the bigger job.

Why It Matters

Why Not Just Leave the Stump?

In South Florida specifically, a left stump causes problems that don't show up in cooler climates.

🐝

Termites

A decaying stump in Broward County is an active termite food source sitting in your yard. Subterranean termites work underground, and a stump near the house is an invitation.

🍄

Fungus and Rot

Constant humidity and rainfall mean stumps here break down wet. Mushrooms, fungal mats, and soft rot spread into surrounding soil and nearby plantings.

🌿

Persistent Regrowth

Many South Florida species sprout aggressively from a cut stump. Ficus in particular will send up shoots for years, and cutting them back doesn't stop it.

⚠

Trip Hazard

A ground-level stump disappears into grass within a month. It's a genuine hazard for kids, guests, and anyone running a mower.

🔨

Mowing Obstacle

Every mow becomes a maneuver, and eventually somebody hits it and damages a blade or a deck.

🏠

Resale

Stumps read as deferred maintenance to buyers and inspectors, and they raise questions about why the tree came down in the first place.

The Practical Details

What Happens to the Hole and the Chips?

Grinding produces considerably more chips than people expect — the wood expands as it's shredded, so a modest stump can generate a surprisingly large mound.

You've got two options and we'll do either. We can rake the chips back into the void, which fills it level and costs nothing extra, though the material settles over the following months and usually needs topping off with soil. Or we haul the chips away and backfill with clean topsoil, which gives you a ready-to-seed surface immediately.

One thing to plan for: wood chips tie up nitrogen as they decompose, so grass planted directly into a chip-filled hole tends to come in yellow and thin. If you want lawn there quickly, the topsoil option is the one to take.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How deep do you grind a stump?

Standard grinding goes six to eight inches below grade, which is deep enough to put down soil and grass and never think about it again. If you're planting a new tree, setting a fence post, or laying pavers over the spot, we grind twelve to eighteen inches instead. Tell us what's going in that space and we'll set the depth accordingly.

Does stump grinding remove the roots?

No. Grinding removes the stump and the flare where it meets the ground, but the lateral roots stay in the soil and break down naturally over several years. That's fine for lawn, mulch beds, and general landscaping. If roots are actively lifting a driveway or a wall, that's a separate excavation conversation.

Will the tree grow back after grinding?

Not from a properly ground stump, since the growing tissue is removed along with it. What can happen with aggressive species like ficus is suckering from remaining lateral roots, showing up as small shoots in the lawn nearby. Mowing generally handles them, and they fade as the roots die off.

How long does stump grinding take?

Most residential stumps take 30 minutes to two hours depending on size, wood density, and access. A large ficus or oak stump can run longer. Grinding is usually a same-visit job — we're not coming back a second day for a typical residential stump.

Can you grind a stump close to my house or pool?

Usually, yes, and it's a common request in Miramar where lots are tight. It takes more care, and we check for irrigation lines, utilities, and pool plumbing before starting, since a grinder wheel does not distinguish between wood and PVC. Stumps directly against a foundation or pool shell sometimes need hand work rather than a machine, and we'll tell you upfront if that's the case.

Do you fill in the hole afterward?

We rake the chips back in and level it at no extra charge, which is what most homeowners choose. If you want clean topsoil instead so you can seed or sod right away, we can haul the chips off and backfill — that's a small additional cost and worth it if you want lawn there soon, since fresh chips make grass struggle.
