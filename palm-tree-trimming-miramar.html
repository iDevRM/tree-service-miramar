The Work Itself

What Does Palm Tree Trimming Involve?

Palm trimming means removing dead and dying fronds, cutting off seed pods and flower stalks, and clearing the loose fiber that builds up around the crown. It is not the same job as trimming a shade tree, and a crew that treats it that way will damage the palm.

Palms don't heal wounds the way hardwoods do. A hardwood compartmentalizes damage and grows over it. A palm has a single growing point at the top — the bud — and everything the palm will ever be comes out of it. Cut into it, and the palm dies. There is no recovery and no second chance.

So the work is conservative on purpose. Dead fronds come off, seed pods come off, and green fronds stay unless there's a real reason. We work Miramar's coconut palms, royal palms, queen palms, and sabal palms, plus the foxtails and Christmas palms that showed up in every development built after about 2000.

The Big Mistake

Why Is the “Hurricane Cut” Bad for Palms?

Every year around May, crews go door to door in Broward offering a hurricane cut — stripping the palm back to a narrow vertical plume of fronds pointing straight up. It looks tidy, it feels like storm preparation, and it is genuinely harmful.

Green fronds are how a palm feeds itself. Strip them and the palm draws on stored nutrients to push out replacements, which weakens the trunk it is growing at that moment. That damage shows up later as a narrowed section of trunk called pencil-pointing, and a narrowed trunk is a weak point that snaps in exactly the wind the cut was supposed to protect against.

The accepted standard is not to remove green fronds growing above horizontal — picture a clock face and leave everything above the 9-and-3 line. A palm trimmed to that standard looks fuller than your neighbor's hurricane cut, and it is measurably more likely to still be standing after a storm.

If a company's pitch is that they'll cut your palms way back for hurricane season, that tells you what you need to know about them.

Know the Signs

When Do Miramar Palms Need Trimming?

Most palms here need attention once or twice a year. These are the triggers.

🍂

Coconuts Overhead

A mature coconut weighs several pounds and falls from thirty feet. Over a driveway, patio, or walkway it's a genuine hazard and a liability question.

🌿

Brown Fronds Hanging Down

Dead fronds that have folded against the trunk are wind debris waiting to happen, and they're where rats and wasps nest.

🌼

Heavy Seed Pods and Flower Stalks

Seed production pulls energy from the palm and drops a mess across your yard and pool. Removing pods early keeps both under control.

🐝

Pests in the Crown

Palm weevils and rodents nest in accumulated dead material. Clearing the crown removes the habitat before it becomes an infestation.

🌪

Before Hurricane Season

Dead fronds and loose pods become projectiles. Removing them — not the green fronds — is real storm preparation.

💧

Yellowing From the Bottom Up

Often potassium or magnesium deficiency rather than a trimming issue. We'll say so instead of cutting off fronds the palm still needs.

Local Rules

Are Palm Trees Protected in Broward County?

Broadly, yes. Palms in Broward are generally protected in the same framework as other trees, and in most municipalities routine removal of dead fronds is fine while anything beyond that runs into permitting.

The sabal palm deserves specific mention: it's Florida's state tree and carries additional protection. Removing one is not something to do casually or on the advice of a crew knocking on doors.

Broward also requires that tree work be done by licensed trimmers. That requirement exists partly because so much palm damage in this county traces back to unlicensed crews doing hurricane cuts. We're licensed and insured, and we handle permitting for work that needs it.

Pricing Explained

What Does Palm Trimming Cost in Miramar?

Palm trimming in Miramar typically runs $75 to $150 per palm for smaller species like Christmas and pygmy date palms, and $150 to $400 for tall coconut, royal, and queen palms that require climbing or a lift.

Height is the main driver. A palm a crew can reach with a pole saw from the ground is quick. A forty-foot royal palm means a climber with spikes-free gear or a bucket truck, and the price reflects the equipment and the risk.

Most Miramar properties have several palms, and per-palm pricing drops meaningfully when we're doing four or five in one visit rather than one. If you're on a regular schedule, twice-yearly service costs less per visit than calling only when things get bad.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should palm trees be trimmed in Miramar?

Once or twice a year covers most palms here. Coconut palms usually need twice-yearly attention because of the nuts and the constant seed production. Royal palms self-shed their fronds, so they need less frequent work but the fronds they drop are enormous and heavy. Sabal palms hold dead fronds indefinitely and mainly need periodic cleanup.

Should I get a hurricane cut before storm season?

No. The hurricane cut is one of the most damaging things done to palms in South Florida. Stripping green fronds forces the palm to draw on stored energy, which narrows the trunk it's growing at that time and creates a weak point that snaps in high wind. Real storm prep means removing dead fronds, seed pods, and coconuts, while leaving green fronds above horizontal in place.

Do you remove coconuts from palms?

Yes, and it's one of the most common calls we get in Miramar. A falling coconut can damage a car, a screen enclosure, or a person. We remove existing nuts and the flower stalks that produce the next round, which keeps the palm from re-loading a few months later.

Can trimming kill a palm tree?

It can, in two ways. Cutting into the growing bud at the top kills the palm outright with no possibility of recovery, since a palm has only that one growing point. Over-trimming green fronds doesn't kill it immediately but starves and weakens it over time. This is why palm work should go to someone who does it regularly rather than a general landscaper with a chainsaw.

Why are my palm fronds turning yellow?

Usually a nutrient issue rather than a trimming issue. Potassium deficiency is extremely common in South Florida soil and shows as yellowing and spotting on the oldest, lowest fronds first. Cutting those fronds off makes it worse, because the palm is actively pulling nutrients out of them. The fix is proper palm fertilizer, and we'll tell you that instead of selling you a trim.

Do I need a permit to trim palms in Miramar?

Removing dead fronds is generally maintenance and doesn't require one. Anything that meaningfully reduces a palm, and removal of any kind, runs into Broward and Miramar tree regulations — and sabal palms carry extra protection as Florida's state tree. We check before we cut and handle the paperwork when it's required.
