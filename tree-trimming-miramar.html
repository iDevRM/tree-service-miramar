The Work Itself

What Does Tree Trimming Actually Involve?

Trimming is selective. A crew works through the canopy removing specific limbs for a specific reason — dead wood, crossing branches rubbing bark off each other, growth crowding a roof or driveway, or a canopy so dense that wind can't pass through it.

There are three cuts that matter in South Florida. Crown cleaning takes out dead, dying, and diseased wood. Crown thinning opens the interior so wind moves through instead of pushing against a solid wall of leaves. Crown raising lifts the lower limbs off your roof, fence, or the sidewalk.

What we don't do is top trees. Cutting a canopy flat across the top is the fastest way to kill a mature tree and the surest way to make it dangerous. Topped trees respond with weak, fast vertical shoots that snap in the first real storm.

Know the Signs

When Does a Miramar Tree Need Trimming?

Most trees here need attention every two to three years. These are the situations that move it up the list.

🌳

Branches Touching the Roof

Limbs resting on shingles wear through them and give rodents and roof rats a bridge into your attic. Broward homes see this constantly with oaks and ficus.

🌪

Dense, Unthinned Canopy

A solid canopy catches wind like a sail. Thinning lets gusts pass through, which is the single biggest thing you can do to keep a tree standing through a storm.

💀

Dead Wood in the Crown

South Florida humidity breaks down dead limbs fast. Gray, brittle branches with no leaves will come down — the only question is whether it happens on your schedule or the weather's.

⚡

Growth Toward Power Lines

Anything approaching a service drop is a fire and outage risk. FPL handles primary lines, but the drop from the pole to your house is generally the homeowner's responsibility.

🗺

Blocking Sidewalks or Driveways

Miramar code requires clearance over public walkways. Low limbs over a sidewalk are a liability if someone walks into one.

🌞

Nothing Grows Underneath

A canopy so thick that grass dies beneath it is a canopy that needs thinning. Filtered light through a properly thinned tree lets a lawn survive.

Timing

When Is the Best Time to Trim in South Florida?

Late winter through early spring — roughly February into April — is the window most arborists here prefer. The tree is growing slowly, wounds close cleanly, and you're well ahead of hurricane season.

The practical version: get structural trimming done before June 1. Once storms start rolling through, every tree company in Broward is booked and prices reflect it. Homeowners who call in late August are competing with everyone else who waited.

Dead wood is the exception. Dead limbs come out any time of year, because a dead branch isn't going to get healthier and it isn't going to wait for a convenient season to fall.

Doing It Right

Why Does Over-Trimming Make Trees More Dangerous?

This is the part homeowners rarely hear before they've already paid for it. A tree stripped of most of its canopy is a tree that has lost most of its energy production, and it responds by pushing out fast vertical growth called water sprouts. Those shoots attach weakly, grow quickly, and break easily.

The rule professionals work to is roughly a quarter of the live canopy in any one season, and less than that on a mature or stressed tree. A crew offering to “really open it up” for a flat rate is usually about to do damage you'll be paying for in five years.

It also matters where the cut lands. Cuts made outside the branch collar — that slight swelling where a limb meets the trunk — seal over. Flush cuts into the trunk don't, and in this climate an open wound invites decay fungus within a season.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should trees be trimmed in Miramar, FL?

Most established shade trees in Miramar do well on a two to three year cycle. Fast growers like ficus and black olive often need attention annually, especially near a roof or fence line. Palms follow a different schedule entirely, usually once or twice a year for dead fronds and seed pods. We'll tell you what your specific trees need rather than putting everything on the same plan.

Do I need a permit to trim a tree in Miramar?

Routine maintenance trimming generally does not require a permit, but Miramar regulates work that meaningfully alters a tree's canopy, and the city takes canopy preservation seriously. Hard reductions and anything approaching a removal will need approval. Broward County also requires that tree work be performed by licensed trimmers. We handle the permit question as part of the estimate so you're not guessing.

What's the difference between trimming and pruning?

In practice the two words get used interchangeably, and both refer to selectively removing branches. If there's a distinction people draw, trimming tends to describe shaping and clearance work while pruning describes removing wood for the health of the tree. Either way, what matters is that cuts land at the branch collar and no more than about a quarter of the live canopy comes off at once.

Will trimming help my tree survive a hurricane?

Proper thinning genuinely helps, because it lets wind pass through the canopy instead of pushing against it. Removing dead wood helps too, since dead limbs are the first thing to become airborne. What doesn't help is aggressive over-trimming, which weakens the tree and produces brittle regrowth. Structural thinning done well before June is the version that works.

Do you clean up all the debris?

Yes, hauling and disposal is included in every quote unless you specifically ask us to leave chips for mulch, which some homeowners want. We rake and blow the work area before leaving. You should not be finding branch pieces in your yard the next morning.

Can you trim a tree that's touching my neighbor's property?

You're generally entitled to trim growth that crosses onto your property, up to the property line, without your neighbor's permission. You cannot cross onto their land to do it, and you cannot damage the tree's health in the process. When a tree straddles a line or the situation looks like it could turn into a dispute, we recommend a conversation with the neighbor first.
